seabed-sediment.bin — worldwide seabed sediment map sampled on a 0.1 degree grid

Changes made
  The 55,923 polygons of the map were sampled at the nodes of a 0.1 degree grid (the polygon that contains a
  node gives its class), and the 30 legend classes found in the file were merged into 13 groups. Written by
  tools/seabed-grid.py, which documents the byte format; read by src/data/seabedgrid.js. The app reads the map
  itself, polygon by polygon, from the Shom map service when it is online; this grid is the offline fallback.

Classes (legend codes of the map in brackets)
  0 no data (land, seas beyond 72 degrees of latitude)
  1 rock (NFRoche)                        2 pebbles (NFC, NFCG, NFCS, NFCV)      3 gravel (NFG, NFGC)
  4 sandy or muddy gravel (NFGS, NFGSV, NFGVS, NFGV)                             5 gravelly sand (NFSG, NFSGV, NFSC)
  6 sand (NFS)                            7 muddy sand (NFSV, NFSVG, NFSSi)       8 fine sand (NFSF, NFSFG, NFSFC)
  9 muddy fine sand (NFSFV, NFSFSi)      10 sandy mud (NFVS, NFVSF, NFVG, NFVC, NFVSG)
  11 mud (NFV)                           12 silt (NFSi, NFSiA)                   13 clay (NFA, NFASi)

Definitions in the metadata record (grain size)
  pebbles: at least half of the particles above 20 mm; gravel: 2 to 20 mm; sand: 0.5 to 2 mm; fine sand: 0.05 to
  0.5 mm; silt: 0.01 to 0.05 mm; clay: finer than 0.01 mm; mud: 20 % or more finer than 0.05 mm; "muddy": 5 to 20 %
  finer than 0.05 mm; "gravelly": 15 to 50 % gravel.

Limits
  The map is a compilation: a 1972 UNESCO world map of the ocean floor digitised in 1995, improved with published
  sediment maps generalised to 1:500 000. It shows the sediment province, not the ground at a site, and its
  detail varies from region to region. It describes the top of the seabed only. Not for navigation.
